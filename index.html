Why

为什么不直接让 AI 写？Why not just ask an AI?

通用 AI 的稿子彼此相似：先铺背景，用形容词代替机制，并排罗列组件。获奖论文不是这样写的。skill 把这些差别量化成规则，并逐条检查你的草稿。

Generic AI drafts look alike: background first, adjectives instead of mechanisms, parallel component lists. Award papers do not read that way. The skill turns the differences into measurable rules and checks every draft against them.

Large language models have achieved remarkable success across a wide range of natural language processing tasks. However, as models continue to grow in size, fully fine-tuning all model parameters for each downstream task becomes increasingly impractical, making it prohibitively expensive to deploy multiple fine-tuned instances. In this paper, we propose Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), a novel and efficient approach that freezes the pre-trained model weights and injects trainable low-rank decomposition matrices into each layer of the Transformer architecture, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LoRA achieves comparable or even better performance than full fine-tuning on RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2, and GPT-3, while reducing trainable parameters by 10,000 times and GPU memory by 3 times, and introducing no additional inference latency. We also provide an empirical investigation into rank-deficiency in language model adaptation, providing insights into the effectiveness of LoRA. Our code and models are publicly available.

Serving one language model on many tasks means keeping one adapted copy per task, and at the scale of GPT-3 175B each copy is as large as the model itself. Adapter layers shrink the copy but run in sequence with the model, which adds inference latency, while prefix tuning pays for its trainable prefix with part of the input sequence. We observe that the weight change during adaptation has a low intrinsic rank, so a pair of small matrices can represent it. LoRA freezes the pre-trained weights, trains one such pair for each layer, and merges their product into the original weights at deployment. On GPT-3 175B, this efficient recipe cuts trainable parameters by 10,000 times and GPU memory by 3 times relative to fine-tuning with Adam, and it matches or exceeds fine-tuning quality on RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3 with no added inference latency. A study of rank deficiency in adaptation explains why such small matrices are enough.

普通提示词Plain prompt不使用 skillNo skill

In recent years, large pre-trained language models have achieved remarkable success across a wide range of natural language processing tasks. Many real-world applications rely on adapting a single pre-trained model to multiple downstream tasks, which is typically done through fine-tuning. However, as models become larger and larger, fine-tuning all of the parameters has become increasingly challenging. For example, GPT-3 175B requires storing and deploying a separate copy of 175 billion parameters for each task, making it prohibitively expensive.

To tackle this challenge, many researchers have proposed parameter-efficient adaptation methods, such as adapter layers and prefix tuning. While these methods are effective in reducing the number of trainable parameters, they still suffer from several limitations. Adapter layers introduce additional inference latency, whereas prefix tuning is difficult to optimize and reduces the sequence length available for downstream tasks, which significantly limits their practicality in real-world scenarios.

In this paper, we propose Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), a novel and efficient method that addresses these limitations. Inspired by the finding that over-parameterized models have a low intrinsic dimension, we hypothesize that the weight updates during adaptation also have a low intrinsic rank. Specifically, LoRA freezes the pre-trained weights and injects trainable low-rank matrices into each layer, thereby greatly reducing the number of trainable parameters, making it possible to switch between tasks efficiently while introducing no additional inference latency.

check_draft · 2 warnings8 notes泛泛的开头；缺口只说"若干局限"；洞察被降格为假设generic opening; "several limitations"; the insight is downgraded to a hypothesis
bestpaper-writingbestpaper-writing使用 skillWith the skill

Many applications adapt one large pre-trained language model to several downstream tasks, and full fine-tuning is the standard recipe. It updates every parameter, so each task ends up with its own copy of the model. The cost grows with the model. For GPT-3 175B, deploying many independent fine-tuned instances, each with 175B parameters, is prohibitively expensive.

Prior work reduces this cost in two ways, and each pays for it elsewhere. Adapter layers (Houlsby et al., 2019) insert small trainable modules between Transformer blocks, but the modules must be processed one after another, so every query pays extra latency that is hard to hide when the batch is small. Prefix tuning (Li and Liang, 2021) instead trains a prefix of the input, which is difficult to optimize and occupies part of the sequence length that the task could otherwise use.

Our approach starts from a property of the problem. Over-parameterized models reside on a low intrinsic dimension (Aghajanyan et al., 2020), so the change in weights during adaptation should also have a low intrinsic rank. LoRA turns this into an efficient method: we freeze the pre-trained weights and represent the update of each weight matrix by the product of two small matrices of rank r, trained directly. At deployment the product is added to the frozen weights, so inference runs on the same architecture as the original model and adds no latency, and switching tasks means swapping the two small matrices.

  • 任务：把一个大型预训练语言模型适配到多个下游任务。
  • 现有方法：全量微调（每个任务一份完整副本，GPT-3 175B 的部署代价极高）；Adapter（串行计算，增加推理延迟）；Prefix tuning（难优化，占用序列长度）。
  • 核心观察：过参数化模型位于低本征维度，适配时的权重变化也应有低本征秩。
  • 方法：冻结预训练权重，为每层训练一对低秩矩阵，部署时合并进原权重。
  • 结果：相对 GPT-3 175B + Adam 微调，可训练参数约少 10,000 倍，GPU 显存约少 3 倍；在 RoBERTa、DeBERTa、GPT-2、GPT-3 上持平或更好；无额外推理延迟。
  • 分析：对适配中的秩亏现象做了研究；开源代码与模型。
  • Task: adapt one large pre-trained language model to several downstream tasks.
  • Prior approaches: full fine-tuning (a full copy per task, prohibitive for GPT-3 175B); adapters (sequential, add inference latency); prefix tuning (hard to optimize, takes sequence length).
  • Key observation: over-parameterized models sit on a low intrinsic dimension, so the weight change in adaptation should have a low intrinsic rank.
  • Method: freeze the pre-trained weights, train a pair of low-rank matrices per layer, merge at deployment.
  • Results: about 10,000× fewer trainable parameters and about 3× less GPU memory than fine-tuning GPT-3 175B with Adam; on par or better on RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3; no added inference latency.
  • Analysis: a study of rank deficiency in adaptation; code and checkpoints released.

Workflow

它是怎么写的How it writes

不是一条"写得像论文"的提示词，而是一套先想清楚、再动笔、最后自检的流程。

Not a "sound like a paper" prompt, but a process: think first, write second, check last.

补全缺项Fill the gaps

必需信息缺了就先问，只问一次；问不到的写成 [TODO]，不编造。

Ask once for missing required fields; what cannot be answered becomes [TODO], never an invention.

提炼主张Distil the claim

一句话主张、矛盾类型、关于问题本身的洞察、最相近工作、头条数字。

One-line claim, tension type, the insight about the problem, closest work, headline numbers.

选论证路线Pick the route

八类论文（方法改进、新能力、规模规律、再检验、机理、理论、数据集、测量）各有一条路线。

Eight paper types, each with its own line of argument.

骨架与范例Skeleton and exemplars

每段先写一句要点；按段落功能检索获奖论文的同类段落，学顺序和节奏，不抄句子。

One line per paragraph; retrieve award-paper paragraphs with the same function to learn order and rhythm, never to copy.

起草Draft

摘要和引言先写；方法按"需要 → 机制 → 性质"，实验的消融与主张一一对应。

Abstract and introduction first; methods as need, mechanism, property; ablations that mirror the claims.

自检与报告Check and report

check_draft.py 对照语料统计检查；同时给出 report.md：主张-证据表、待补清单、留给 Limitations 的要点。

check_draft.py compares against corpus statistics; report.md lists the claim-evidence map, TODOs and notes for the Limitations section.

Evaluation

实测：同样的输入，用与不用 skillTested: same input, with and without the skill

只给模型三篇公开论文的摘要（不给任何其他信息），让 4 个模型各用"普通提示"和"使用 skill"分别写出标题、摘要、引言、方法、实验，直接写进 CVPR 作者模板，共 24 份草稿；再匿名打乱，由 3 位评委（Sonnet、Opus、GPT-Astra）按同一量表打分。

Each model received only the abstract of three public papers (nothing else) and wrote the title, abstract, introduction, method and experiments straight into the CVPR author kit, once with a plain prompt and once with the skill: 24 drafts. They were anonymised, shuffled and scored by three judges (Sonnet, Opus, GPT-Astra) on one rubric.

怎么读这些数字How to read this

  • 可以说：3 位评委合并后，使用 skill 的草稿在 D1–D8 上更高，4 个模型都是如此（单个评委、单个模型的格子里并非处处如此，见左表）；方法与实验（D9）的提升最大，因为缺失信息被写成 [TODO]，而不是编造的细节。
  • 不能说：skill 保证不编造。编造量随模型而异：GPT-Astra、GPT-Sol 接近 0，Opus、Sonnet 在使用 skill 后反而更多。
  • 不给模型排名：评委之间并不一致（Sonnet 与 GPT-Astra 的排名几乎不相关，Opus 评委把 Opus 的 skill 稿排得最高，可能有自我偏好），模型的先后取决于谁来评。
  • What holds: pooled over the three judges, skill drafts score higher on D1–D8 for all four models (not every single judge-by-model cell, see the tables). The largest gain is in method and experiments (D9), because missing information becomes [TODO] rather than invented detail.
  • What does not: the skill does not guarantee zero fabrication. It varies by model: GPT-Astra and GPT-Sol stay near 0, while Opus and Sonnet fabricate more with the skill than without.
  • No model ranking: the judges disagree (Sonnet and GPT-Astra rankings are nearly uncorrelated, and the Opus judge ranks Opus skill drafts highest, a possible self-preference), so the order of models depends on who judges.

局限：只有 3 篇论文、每格一次生成，属探索性比较而非统计结论；使用 skill 的草稿带有 [TODO]，评委能认出，并非严格盲评；F 由评委自行统计，未逐条人工核对。

Caveats: three papers and one generation per cell, so this is an exploratory comparison, not a statistical result; skill drafts carry [TODO] markers the judges can recognise, so scoring was not strictly blind; F was counted by the judges and not hand-verified.
